Validating transient-detection pipelines

During my postdoctoral work at CPPM, I was a member of the LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC), where I contributed to the validation of the LSST software stack using simulated DESC data and to supernova analyses based on the DC2 data challenge.

My work focused on image-processing validation and Difference Image Analysis (DIA), a key technique for detecting variable and transient sources by comparing observations acquired at different epochs.

I also tested the processing chain on real Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) observations from the COSMOS UDEEP field. This work included astrometric and photometric calibration to account for instrumental and optical effects and to improve the consistency of difference images and the resulting supernova light curves.

Objective. Validate and improve the reliability of the image-processing chain used to detect transient sources and extract scientifically usable light curves from large astronomical surveys.

Instrument context

Continuation of the transient-classification work

The work initiated during my time at CPPM was later continued by the collaboration after my departure. The scientific objective remained closely related: improving the reliability of transient-candidate classification in large astronomical surveys.

The methodology subsequently evolved substantially. The later study developed a deep-learning approach for Real-Bogus classification using simulated transient injections, without relying on human-labelled training samples, and incorporated uncertainty quantification into the classification process.

Scientific continuity. This later work follows the same broader objective as the activity I initiated: making transient-detection pipelines more reliable and scalable. The technical approach, however, was extensively redesigned and developed after my departure from the project.
